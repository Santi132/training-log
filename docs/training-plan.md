# Full-Body Training Plan

**Goals (in order):** strength, stability, then flexibility/mobility. Glute and leg development is the main focus.
**Frequency:** at least 3 days a week, aiming for 4
**Equipment:** dumbbells, barbell, kettlebells, cable machine, jump rope, stairmaster or treadmill
**Starting point:** 5 strict pull-ups, 35 push-ups, 3 × 12 pike push-ups
**Areas to protect:** lower back, slight knee pain, cervical and thoracic pain

> Assumes prior lifting experience and that you've already seen a physical therapist. Where your PT's
> guidance differs from anything here, follow the PT. Swap in their prescribed exercises (especially
> warm-up and knee/neck work) for the equivalents below.

---

## 1. How this plan accounts for your pain areas

### Lower back

- **No heavy barbell back squat.** The squat pattern uses goblet squats, double-KB front squats, and
  split squats instead. These put less load on the spine and need more core bracing.
- **Lighter hinges done well.** DB/KB RDLs instead of heavy barbell RDLs. Hip thrusts and cable
  pull-throughs give the glutes heavy work with very little load on the spine.
- **The McGill Big 3** (modified curl-up, side plank, bird dog) is in the warm-up. These are
  well-researched exercises for building spine stability.
- **Avoid loaded spinal flexion**, such as sit-ups or weighted crunches. Core work is anti-movement
  (resisting being bent or twisted) plus controlled leg raises.
- **Brace on every lift:** breathe into your belly and sides, then tighten as if about to be poked.

### Knees

- **Reverse lunges instead of forward lunges.** Reverse lunges put less shear force on the knee.
- **Box goblet squats** let you control depth. Start at a depth that doesn't hurt and lower the box over time.
- **Knee-strengthening accessories:** tibialis raises, slow eccentric step-downs, and Spanish squats or
  wall sits (isometric holds that often reduce knee pain).
- **Jump rope:** low hops, soft landings, short bouts. Swap in stairmaster time if your knees complain.

### Neck and upper back

- **Neck position:** keep a slight chin tuck in push-ups, planks, and pull-ups. Don't reach your chin over
  the bar or let your head sag in planks.
- **Pike push-ups:** let your head travel *in front of* your hands to form a tripod, and keep your neck
  neutral. No handstand push-up work until your neck is symptom-free for a few weeks.
- **Overhead pressing:** use the landmine press (barbell in a corner) or a half-kneeling single-arm DB
  press. Both are easier on the neck and thoracic spine than strict barbell overhead presses.
- **Daily upper-back care:** chin tucks, thoracic extensions over a foam roller, and
  face pulls / Y-T-Ws. These are in the warm-ups.
- **Shrugs and heavy carries:** keep the load moderate if they irritate your neck.

### Pain-monitoring rule

During and after an exercise, **pain up to 3/10 that settles within 24 hours is acceptable.** If pain is above
that, or lingers into the next day, reduce the load, range of motion, or tempo, or swap the exercise for its
listed alternative.

## 2. Weekly structure

Run the sessions as a **rotation, not on fixed days**. Do the next session in the list whenever you can
train.

| Session | Focus                                             | When                         |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **A**   | Squat pattern + horizontal push/pull              | Every week                   |
| **B**   | Hinge pattern + vertical push/pull                | Every week                   |
| **C**   | Glutes + single-leg + stability                   | Every week                   |
| **D**   | Conditioning + glute/stability/mobility (lighter) | 4th day, when you can fit it |

- **3-day week:** A → B → C (e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri)
- **4-day week:** A → B → rest → C → D (e.g. Mon / Tue / Thu / Sat)
- **Busy week (2 days):** do A and C. Add 3 sets of pike push-ups to C.
- Try not to do A/B/C on back-to-back days more than once a week. D can follow anything.

**Session length:** about 75–85 min for A/B/C including the 25-minute warm-up and cardio (A runs a little
longer with its swing intervals). About 60–65 min for D.

## 3. Warm-up (every session, \~25–28 min)

Go from general to specific: get warm, mobilize, stabilize the spine, prep each joint with bands, prep for
the session's main lifts, then ramp up into the first working set. Banded work is light and smooth, with
no shrugging on the shoulder drills. Run the knee and shoulder drills past your PT and swap in anything
they've prescribed.

**Short on time:** drop block 1 or halve block 5 (\~20 min). The minimum is blocks 2, 3, 4 and 6 (\~18 min).

### Block 1: Raise temperature (3 min)

Easy incline walk or stairmaster. Jump rope with low hops is fine if your knees are happy.

### Block 2: Mobility (\~5 min)

| Foam roller thoracic extensions                                       | 8–10        | Thoracic mobility |
| 90/90 hip switches                                                    | 6/side      | Hip rotation     |
| Banded hip flexor stretch (band at front of hip, anchored behind, half-kneeling) | 30–45s/side | Hip flexors, eases the lower back |

| Exercise       | Dose            | Purpose                 |
| -------------- | --------------- | ----------------------- |
| McGill curl-up | 3 × 10s holds   | Spine stability         |

2–3 lighter sets of your first lift to groove the pattern (Session D: one light round of the complex).

**KB complex** (do all reps of each move, then go straight to the next without setting the bell down):

1. KB swing (2-hand, hip hinge, not a squat) × 10
2. Goblet squat × 8
3. Single-arm clean × 5/side
4. Half-kneeling or standing press × 5/side. Skip if your neck flares; do rows instead.
5. Reverse lunge, goblet hold × 5/side

If your back is irritated: swap swings for KB deadlifts and keep the bell lighter.

## 5. Calisthenics progressions

### Pull-ups (current: 5 strict)

Goal: 10 strict, then weighted. Use **frequent submaximal sets**: many sets of a few reps, never to failure.

| Weeks | Session A                                                      | Session C              | Optional "grease the groove"                       |
| ----- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–2   | 5 × 3 + 2 × slow negatives (5s)                                | 4 × 3                  | 1–2 reps, several times a day, on non-lifting days |
| 3–4   | 5 × 4 + 2 × negatives                                          | 5 × 3                  | Same                                               |
| 5–6   | 4 × 5                                                          | 6 × 3, 2s pause at top | Same                                               |
| 7     | Deload: 3 × 3                                                  | 3 × 3                  | Rest                                               |
| 8+    | Retest max. If 8+, start ladders (1-2-3-4) and work toward 10. |                        |                                                    |

Chin-ups on Session B count as lighter volume. Once you can do 3 × 8 strict, start adding a DB between your feet.

### Push-ups (current: 35)

You've outgrown basic push-ups for strength, so make them harder rather than doing more reps:
**deficit → 2s paused → 3-1-1 tempo → archer → weighted (plate on upper back or a weighted vest)**.
Stay in roughly 8–15 reps per set. Move up a level when you hit 3 × 15.

### Pike push-ups (current: 3 × 12)

You're ready for **feet-elevated pike push-ups** (feet on a 12–18" box). Progress the box height, then do
deficit pikes (hands on plates). Because of your neck, **stay at the elevated pike level** until your
neck has been symptom-free for 3–4 weeks before trying wall handstand push-up negatives.

### Core

- **Planks:** plank → RKC plank → long-lever plank → plank with shoulder taps
- **Dead bug:** bodyweight → holding a KB overhead → banded or cable-resisted
- **Hanging:** knee raise → tuck-and-extend → straight-leg raise. Toes-to-bar only if your back tolerates it.
- **Side plank:** knees → full → Copenhagen (short lever, knee on bench) → Copenhagen (long lever)

## 6. Progression and periodization

**Method: double progression.** Each exercise has a rep range (e.g. 8–12). When you hit the top of the range
on all sets with good form, increase the weight (DB +5 lb, barbell +5–10 lb) or move up a calisthenics level.
Then work back up the range.

**Finding starting weights:** base them on your current working weights, roughly 10% lighter for the
first week. Familiar lifts done at a new frequency (3–4× per week) need a week to settle in, and some
exercises here (goblet box squat, landmine press) may be new loads for you.

On accessories and isolation work (face pulls, kickbacks, abduction, rows), take the last set to
0–1 RIR. Keep the main lifts at 1–3 RIR because you train them again within 48 hours.

| Weeks | Phase      | Effort                                                                          |
| ----- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Calibrate  | 2–3 RIR. Find working weights for the new exercises, check how pain responds.   |
| 2–3   | Build      | 2–3 RIR                                                                         |
| 4–6   | Push       | 1–2 RIR on main lifts. Add weight when you hit the top of ranges.               |
| 7     | **Deload** | Same exercises, \~50% of the sets, 3–4 RIR. Keep the cardio easy.               |
| 8–13  | Block 2    | Repeat with heavier loads and harder calisthenics levels. Optional swaps below. |

**Barbell swaps** (use from block 2, or from day 1 if you already lift these pain-free):

- Goblet box squat → double-KB front squat or barbell front squat
- DB RDL → barbell RDL (light, form first)
- Reverse lunge → front-foot-elevated split squat
- Push-ups → weighted push-ups

## 7. Weekly cardio and conditioning

| Type                                | Where                | Weekly total        |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------- |
| Zone 2 (incline walk / stairmaster) | End of A, B, C       | \~40 min            |
| Intervals (jump rope)               | Session B, Session D | \~10 min            |
| KB swing intervals                  | Session A            | 8–10 rounds (\~10 min) |
| KB complexes                        | Session D            | 4–5 rounds          |
| Optional                            | Walk on rest days    | As much as you like |

Do cardio **after** lifting so your strength work isn't fatigued. On a 3-day week with no Session D, add
3–4 rounds of the KB complex at the end of Session C, before the incline walk.

## 8. Daily 5-minute routine (rest days or mornings)

Aimed at your back, neck, and glutes. This frequent, light work often helps more than anything in the gym.

1. Chin tucks: 10 × 3s hold
2. Foam roller thoracic extensions: 10
3. Couch stretch: 45s/side
4. Glute bridge hold: 2 × 30s
5. Side-lying hip abduction: 15/side
6. Bird dog: 5/side, 5s hold

## 9. Tracking

Log every session. A notes app or spreadsheet is fine.

```
Date | Session | Exercise | Weight/Level | Sets × Reps | RIR | Pain (0-10, where)
```

**Retest every 6 weeks:** max strict pull-ups, max push-ups, max feet-elevated pike push-ups, side plank hold,
and your hip thrust working weight for 10 reps.

### 12-week targets (rough)

- Pull-ups: 5 → 8–10 strict
- Push-ups: weighted or archer push-ups for 3 × 8+
- Pike: feet-elevated pike push-ups, 3 × 10 from a high box
- Hip thrust: a large jump in load with a solid glute contraction at lockout
- Back, knee, and neck pain: lower and less frequent than now
